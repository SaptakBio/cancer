For the sake of simplification and first draft purposes, we will assume that the carcinoma is a single well defined tumor mass with only one(Or none) necrotic core and assuming fat and scaffolding is distributed uniformly throughout the tumor
We will assume that uniform blood flow wrt to the center. That is the blood flow is only a function of the distance from the center of the tumor and distance from the edge
How is the center determined? We are taking the part that recieves the least blood flow as the center of the tumor. This is typically the area that is most hypoxic and has the highest concentration of necrotic tissue. 
The center can be identified through imaging techniques such as MRI or CT scans, which can highlight areas of low perfusion and necrosis. 
Additionally, histological analysis of biopsy samples can provide further confirmation of the tumor's center by identifying regions with the least viable cells and highest levels of necrosis.

Biopsy method:-
We didnt assume that the cells are uniformly distributed throughout the tumour, so we will take multiple biopsy samples from different regions of the tumor to ensure a representative analysis. 

How to take the biopsy? 
Options:-
- Along the chord of the tumor, taking samples at regular intervals to cover the entire mass.
- Radial sampling from the center to the periphery, ensuring that samples are taken from both the hypoxic core and the more vascularized outer regions.
- Grid sampling, where the tumor is divided into a grid pattern and samples are taken from each section to provide a comprehensive overview of the tumor

Grid sampling is more useful and more randomized but it is significanlty more challenging to implement in practice so we will ignore it for now

So we will go with Radial and Chord sampling methods for our biopsy approach. We will compare the results afterwards

Two stage markov chain:-

Instead of one giant transition matrix T(si,sj), we split every step into 2 stages. This is exact, not an approximation.
Technically we could have done it in a single matrix but this is just more interpretable + makes it easier for us to trim the number of parameter needed. 
It is just the chain rule:

Stage 1:- Which event happens to the cell
Events = (Death, Reproduce, Persist, Progress, Transformation, Damage, Repair, Senesce)
- Persist = Same genotype, same phase(H/D/S), age goes up by 1. Persistence always means you age up. So P(Persist) = 0 for the last allowed age of a phase (Hn, Dn, Sm)
- Damage = Hk -> D1. Genotype remains same
- Repair = Dk -> H1. Genotype remains same
- Senesce = Hk -> S1 or Dk -> S1. Genotype remains same
- Transformation = Any transition where the genotype changes. Example H1_A -> D1_B is a Transformation. Not Damage. So is H1_A -> H1_B. This is the only reason Damage, Repair and Senesce dont need a genotype draw
- Reproduce = Parent is gone, children are new cells (tuple)
- Death = Natural death, drug death, mitotic catastrophe, slow death of senescent cell. All of them are Death
Stage 1 is where blood flow, drug concentration, crowding and all that act. This is the part that needs to be fitted. For each (phase, age, genotype) the probabilities of the allowed events sum to 1.
Which events are allowed for which phase is decided by experts. Not allowed = exactly 0.
Note:- Dn must not persist, so it can only die (or repair, senesce, transform, reproduce if allowed). Same for the last age of H and S.

Stage 2:- Given the event, what is the exact next state
- Persist, Death, Damage, Repair, Senesce = No draw needed. Next state is fully decided by the event (Genotype same, age resets to 1 or goes up by 1 as above, Death just means the cell is gone)
- Transformation = Draw the new state from a small table for that parent state. Only entries where genotype changes
- Reproduce = Draw the tuple of children from a small table for that parent state. 
For stage 2 of Transformation and Reproduce also we need to consider the effect of blood flow, drug concentration, crowding and all that. So the tables are conditional on these factors.

T(si,sj) = P(event e | si) * P(sj | si, e) where e is the event that happened to the cell in stage 1

If e is persist,death,damage,repair or senesce then P(sj | si, e) = 1 if sj is the next state of si given the event e and 0 otherwise. So we dont need to draw anything for these events.

After this we can just use the same method as before to get the population of each state

Consider the following example:-
Let's say there are 2 types of treatment A and B. A causes damage to the cell membrane and B causes damage to the DNA. 
Now if a cell has damage to the membrane the's it's probability to die immediately increases to almost 1. 
But if a cell has damage to the DNA, it may die quickly or become senescent but it is not immediately fatal

One way to fix it is to have a vector of damage states instead of a single damage state. But modeling it with markov will be really complex
An easier way is to have a single damage state like usual. Since the transition probabilites depend on the type of treatment, the transition probabilites will already handle the change in death,reproduction and other probabilities without explicitly modeling the type
But this has another issue. That is, even after the treatment is stopped, and the drug is washed off, some of the effects may linger. So the transition probabilites should also depend on a short history of the treatment

Cell to volume:-

Vi_total(t) = Ni*(t) * (Volume of individual cancer cell+Necessary packing) + Ni^(t) * (Volume of individual healthy cell+Necessary packing) + Ri(t) + Si(t) + Ei(t)

Ri(t) = Volume of dead cell debris in grid i at time t
Si(t) = Volume of scaffolding and fat components in grid i at time t
Ei(t) = Due to fluid accumulation and other stuff in grid i at time t

NOTE:Under drug/treatment, the volume of cell is not constant. It can change due to various factors such as cell swelling, shrinkage, or changes in the extracellular matrix. We are taking it constant for now because I doubt gettinf that information is possible. But this is something to keep in mind


As the treatment continues, we do the following :- 
1. Run the model and decide on a suitable treatment
2. Administer the treatment and monitor the tumor's response over time using non-invasive imaging techniques(Taking biopsy again and again is inhuman + this method starts of with a shit ton of biopsy at the start so not a good idea)
3. Compare the predicted outcomes(Only geometric stuff) with the actual outcomes and adjust the model parameters as necessary to improve its accuracy.If prediction is really diffrent from actual outcome, immediate biopsy is needed
4. Repeat. 
5. Sometimes we may also take another biopsy after a few sessions of the treatment to validate the population distribution and also fine tune the model parameters
6. We may need to give them proper recovery time after each treatment session. I am not modeling that part here because no point. It is up to decision of doctor based on established literature and patient condition. But minimal rest is better which will hopefully happen due to the optimization of the treatment by reduced dosage and proper drug mix

Goal with the simulation:-
- Reduce the amount of chemo used to minimize side effects while still effectively targeting the tumor.
- Optimize the treatment schedule to maximize the effectiveness of the therapy while minimizing harm to healthy tissue.
- Understand the dynamics of tumor growth and response to treatment, which can inform future therapeutic strategies and improve patient outcomes.
- Predict the response to different treatment scenarios and identify the most effective approaches and mix of drugs for a given tumor type and patient profile.
